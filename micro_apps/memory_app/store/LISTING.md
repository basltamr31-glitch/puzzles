## Title (max 30 characters)

```
Memory Match: Card Pairs
```

24 characters. "Memory Match" is the term people search for; "Card
Pairs" catches the longer query without keyword-stuffing. The app's own
name stays `Memory Match` — this is the store title only.

## Short description (max 80 characters)

```
400 memory puzzles across 8 themes. Play offline, no account needed.
```

68 characters. Leads with the two things that actually differentiate it:
the volume, and that it works with no network and no sign-up.

## Full description (max 4000 characters)

```
Turn over the cards, find every pair — 400 puzzles, all playable offline.

Memory Match is a calm, uncluttered puzzle game. No account, no sign-up, no internet needed to play. Open it and start.

FOUR DIFFICULTIES THAT ACTUALLY DIFFER
• Easy — 4x3 board, 6 pairs, symbols spread across themes
• Medium — 4x4 board, 8 pairs, symbols spread across themes
• Hard — 6x4 board, 12 pairs, every symbol from one theme
• Expert — 6x5 board, 15 pairs, every symbol from one theme

Pair count alone is the lazy measure of difficulty. A board of a dog, a plane, a pizza and a trumpet is easier to hold in your head than a smaller board of eight different birds — so the symbols themselves tighten as you climb. The gentle tiers mix themes; the hard tiers draw everything from one family, which is what makes twelve cards genuinely hard rather than merely numerous.

THREE LIVES, AND A FAIR RULE ABOUT THEM
You get three mistakes per puzzle — but a mismatch only costs a life if you had already been shown both cards. Turning over two cards you have never seen is not a mistake, it is how the game works. A life is only ever spent on something you actually forgot.

EIGHT THEMES
Animals, food, nature, travel, sports, faces, party and objects. The harder puzzles draw every card from a single theme, so the whole board belongs together — and is that much harder to tell apart.

SIX LANGUAGES
Read the app in English, Arabic, German, French, Spanish or Italian. The puzzles themselves need no translation: a picture of a dog is a picture of a dog everywhere. Arabic menus are fully right-to-left.

EARN YOUR STARS
• Three stars for a clean solve — no mistakes, no hints
• Track puzzles solved, stars earned, and your best run
• Build a day streak by playing on consecutive days
• Six badges to unlock, from your first solve to a hundred

BUILT TO BE PLEASANT
• Tap a card to turn it over — matched pairs stay face up
• Stuck? A hint matches one pair for you. It costs a star, not a life
• Your progress saves itself — close the app mid-puzzle and come back to the same board
• Puzzles unlock in order, so there is always a clear next one
• Move counter and timer, or just play
• Light and dark themes
• Six background music tracks, or silence — sound and vibration both switch off
• Optional gentle reminders if you want a nudge to play

WORKS WITHOUT A CONNECTION
Every puzzle ships inside the app. There is no server, no account and no login. Nothing you do is uploaded — your progress and settings stay on your device. A connection is only ever used for ads.

400 puzzles: 100 per difficulty. Every one was verified by machine to hold each symbol exactly twice, to fill its board, and never to deal a pair into two touching cards — so no puzzle is unsolvable, and none is given away on the first tap.
```

### Screenshots to capture during the device test pass

Six is a good number. Capture in this order — it tells a story to
someone scrolling the listing:

1. **A board mid-game**, with two or three pairs already matched and two
   cards face up — the single most useful shot, because it shows the
   whole mechanic at a glance
2. **The moment a pair matches**, with the match highlight visible
3. **Puzzle list** showing the difficulty banner and progress
4. **Difficulty picker** — communicates depth
5. **The Progress screen**, stars and badges visible — shows there is
   something to come back for
6. **Dark mode**, on any screen

Do not add marketing text over the first screenshot. Play crops and
scales these heavily, and the shot that reads clearly at thumbnail size
is a clean board, not a captioned one.
